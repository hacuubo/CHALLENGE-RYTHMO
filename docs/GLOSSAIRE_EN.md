# English version: translation conventions and glossary

The English version must read as if it had been written from scratch by an English-speaking electrophysiologist and
device specialist. The target register is that of EHRA/HRS educational material and of the ESC guidelines.
Do not produce a word-for-word translation: rephrase freely, but keep **every medical fact, number, unit and nuance**
exactly as in the French. Never add, remove or "correct" medical content during translation. If you think the
French is wrong, translate it faithfully and report the issue separately.

## Style

- Spelling: **British English**, as used by the ESC/EHRA (e.g. *oesophageal*, *haemodynamic*, *programme* as a noun but
  *program* for the device verb, *minimise*, *atrioventricular*, *centre*). Keep the spelling consistent within a question.
- Numbers use a **decimal point** (0.5 V, 2.5 ms) and a normal space between number and unit (0.5 V, 400 ms, 70 bpm).
  Keep the symbols ≥, ≤ and →. Percentages without a space: 35%. Heart rates in bpm; durations in ms, s, min, h.
- Typography: no space before `:`, `;`, `?` or `!`. Use straight quotes `"…"` in place of French guillemets `« … »`.
- Answer comments: start with **"Correct:"** for a right answer and **"Incorrect:"** for a wrong one (French *Juste :* / *Faux :*).
  If a French comment starts differently (e.g. *Oui*, *Non*, *Piège*), use a natural English equivalent.
- True/false questions: the options are exactly `"True"` and `"False"` (French *Vrai* / *Faux*).
- Keep the questions' difficulty unchanged. Options must stay **balanced in length** (the right answer must not become the
  longest one). Keep them short, parallel in structure and free of cues.
- Brand names, algorithm names and trademarks stay in their official English form (Medtronic MVP, Abbott VIP, Boston
  Scientific RYTHMIQ, AV Search+, Biotronik Closed Loop Stimulation, MicroPort SafeR, Capture Management, AutoCapture…).
  French-specific labels used only by the French product literature must be replaced by the international name.
- Trial names, guideline names and reference citations stay unchanged.
- Keep `\n` paragraph breaks where the French has them.

## Conventions settled at the final review (2026-09)

- **Multiple-answer questions**: the French tag *(plusieurs réponses)* is always rendered **"(select all that apply)"**,
  at the end of the stem. Do not use *(multiple answers)* or *(more than one answer)*.
- **"Good habit:"** introduces the practical take-home sentence that the French opens with *Réflexe :* or *Réflexe à avoir :*.
  Avoid the calques *Reflex:*, *Practical reflex:* and the variant *Rule of thumb:*; elsewhere, *premier réflexe* is
  "the first step".
- **program / programme**: the device verb is *program* (program, reprogram, programmed, programming); the noun keeps the
  British *programme* (MRI programme, ATP programme, ETAPES programme). Start options with "Program …", never "Programme …".
- **diathermy** is the single term for *bistouri électrique* (not *electrocautery* or *electrosurgery*):
  "monopolar diathermy", "diathermy interference".
- **backup** is written as one word (backup pulse, backup shock, backup lead, backup pacing); *back up* only as a verb of
  direction ("conducts back up the fast pathway").
- **beat-to-beat** for the compound adjective (beat-to-beat capture verification, beat-to-beat variation); the adverbial
  forms *beat by beat* and *from beat to beat* are acceptable.
- **SNRT / CSNRT** (not *cSNRT*); **burst** and **ramp** for *salve* and *rampe*; **callipers** (British spelling);
  **review screen** for the simulator's *écran de relecture*; **EP recording system** for the *polygraphe*.
- French regulatory content (15/SAMU, ETAPES, HDS, driving-licence decree, Claeys-Leonetti law) stays in the text but is
  framed explicitly as French ("In France…", "15 in France").
